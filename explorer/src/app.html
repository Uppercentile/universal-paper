An open language for agreements · Explorer

An agreement you can compute.

Universal Paper writes agreements between aggregators, suppliers, and members as data with a fingerprint. Every system that holds a copy computes the same answer, and any change shows. Scroll to see how. Everything on this page is computed live in your browser, except the recorded traces under Replay.

1 · THE CLAUSE

This sentence is a clause.

It reads like any contract. It is generated from a Term definition and the values the parties chose. Nobody typed it.

2 · THE DATA

Underneath, it is data.

The clause names a Term by its own fingerprint and fills in its parameters. The language sets no default values. The parties supply every number.

3 · THE FINGERPRINT

The data has one fingerprint.

The agreement is put in canonical form and hashed with SHA-256. Signatures cover this fingerprint, so they cover the text and the data at once.

4 · ONE WORD

Change one word, and the fingerprint flips.

Move the fee from 2% to 2.5%. The text changes, and so does every character position the hash touches. Press the button, or watch it alternate.

5 · EVERYONE CHECKS

Each party computes it alone.

The aggregator’s contract system, the supplier’s ERP, and a member’s spreadsheet each evaluate the signed agreement from their own copy, and land on the same state fingerprint.

6 · A CHANGED COPY

A quietly edited copy is caught.

Change the rate in one copy after signing, and its signatures no longer verify. That copy rejects the event and drops out of agreement with the others, at the exact event that was touched.


        

        

      

    

  


  

    

      Proving ground
      
Random agreements, random attacks, every run checked

      
Each run invents parties, tiers, items, prices, a fee, reporting periods, tier changes, payments (some short, some late), and sometimes an agent with a Mandate or fully sealed values. It then attacks copies of the result and checks eight invariants against independent arithmetic. Runs continue until you close the page. Every run is reproducible from its seed.

Sign it, then change one word, and the signatures stop verifying.
