It is not legal advice, not a legal opinion on any agreement, and it creates no attorney-client relationship. Uppercentile does not give legal advice or review agreements. Whether an agreement is valid, enforceable or compliant depends on the facts, the parties and the law that applies, and is a question for your own counsel.

Universal Paper is an open language for agreements between group purchasing organizations (“aggregators”), suppliers and members. An agreement written in it is a JSON record with a fixed human-readable text, identified by a SHA-256 hash and signed by every party. The specification is an early working draft, UP/0.1-draft, and its Terms have not yet been reviewed by counsel (Overview). Each statement below links to the section of the specification it rests on, so it can be checked against the source.

Does Uppercentile hold our data?

No agreement data. Uppercentile runs no registry, ledger or database of agreements, and none is needed: a person with the specification and standard cryptography can check an agreement’s validity, state and obligations without any server (Overview §3).

For confidential matters, prices and other sensitive values can be sealed before a bundle leaves the parties’ systems, or the MCP server can be run locally so that nothing reaches Uppercentile at all. Electronic signatures: E-SIGN and UETA

Under the federal E-SIGN Act, a signature, contract or other record relating to a transaction in or affecting interstate or foreign commerce may not be denied legal effect, validity or enforceability solely because it is electronic (15 U.S.C. §7001(a)). The Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) says the same at state level (§7), and E-SIGN lets a state’s enactment of UETA govern in its place (15 U.S.C. §7002(a)). California’s enactment is Cal. Civ. Code §1633.1 et seq.; New York instead has its own Electronic Signatures and Records Act (N.Y. State Tech. Law §301 et seq.).

Both statutes define an electronic signature as an electronic sound, symbol or process attached to or logically associated with a record and executed or adopted by a person with the intent to sign it (15 U.S.C. §7006(5); UETA §2(8)). Here is how Universal Paper lines up with each element.

It is associated with exactly one record: change one character and the hash changes, and the signature no longer verifies.Domain §1.2, §1.3

The text ends with a signature block stating that each party signs the agreement identified by that hash.Axiom A8, Inference §7

Two further points of scope. E-SIGN and UETA exclude some subjects, such as wills and most of the Uniform Commercial Code, but both keep UCC Articles 2 and 2A, sales and leases of goods, within their scope (15 U.S.C. §7003(a)(3); UETA §3(b)). And E-SIGN’s consumer consent rules (§7001(c)) apply where a law requires information to be given to a consumer in writing; agreements among GPOs, suppliers and members are agreements between organizations. As the specification itself says, a Universal Paper agreement must still be a valid contract under the applicable law (Overview §7.2).

Software agents that sign for a party

E-SIGN provides that a contract may not be denied legal effect solely because its formation involved the action of electronic agents, so long as the agent’s action is legally attributable to the person to be bound (15 U.S.C. §7001(h)). UETA §14 likewise recognizes contracts formed by electronic agents.

Universal Paper makes that attribution explicit. A party may give software, or a person, a Mandate: a signed record from the principal stating which kinds of action the agent may take, for which roles and agreements, within what limits for each value, and until when. An agent’s signature counts only if a valid, unexpired Mandate covers that exact action. An action outside the Mandate is not a disputed act of the agent: it is invalid by definition and never enters the record (Axiom A9, Domain §10). The Mandate is therefore written evidence of the scope of actual authority. In this draft a Mandate can end only by expiry; there is no revocation (Design notes §4).

Existing contracts: UP:Overlay and the prose agreement

Parties do not have to renegotiate to use Universal Paper. They can describe an existing master agreement with the UP:Overlay Term, which identifies the existing contract by the SHA-256 hash of its document as the parties hold it, along with its title and signing date. Its text, which every party signs, reads:

“This record describes the agreement titled [title], signed on [date], whose document has the hash [hash]. It does not amend or replace that agreement. If this record and that agreement are inconsistent, [the existing agreement / this record] controls.”

  • The parties choose which text controls. The Term’s governs parameter has two values and no default: "document" renders as “the existing agreement controls”, and "this-record" as “this record controls”. The Mapping guide §2 and the published overlay example use "document", so the prose Master Service Agreement prevails in a dispute.
  • It is a contractual statement, not machine logic. UP:Overlay has no clause forms. It changes no state and creates no obligations; it has legal meaning only. Which text controls is for the parties and, in a dispute, for the forum their agreement names (see UP:Governing-Law).
  • It may be partial. Provisions the Term library cannot express, such as indemnities or insurance, stay only in the document. By signing, the parties attest that the overlay accurately describes their contract for what it covers (Mapping guide §2, step 4).
  • No backlog. Duties arise only for periods ending after the overlay is signed, so describing a contract signed years ago creates no overdue obligations (Inference §3.5).
  • Keep the exact file. The hash identifies one document byte for byte, such as the executed PDF. A re-saved or re-scanned copy has a different hash.

Counsel will want to read an overlay alongside the existing agreement’s integration and amendment clauses. The overlay states that it does not amend, and it is drafted to be read that way.

Antitrust: why axiom A6 keeps prices out of the language

Section 1 of the Sherman Act prohibits contracts, combinations and conspiracies in restraint of trade (15 U.S.C. §1). A shared standard used by competitors raises two familiar questions: whether the standard itself carries economic terms that invite coordination, and whether using it exchanges competitively sensitive information among competitors (see United States v. Container Corp. of America, 393 U.S. 333 (1969)).

On the first question, Universal Paper standardizes process, definitions and compliance mechanics, never prices or economic terms (Overview §7.1). Axiom A6 states it as a validity rule:

“The core, every Term definition, and every Mandate format define no default, suggested, or typical value for any price, discount, fee, rate, threshold, volume, quantity, or period. Every such value is supplied by the parties.”

A6 is checkable on the face of the specification, not a policy statement. A Term’s parameters carry only a type and a description, so there is nowhere to put a default. Every amount, rate, period or count in a rule is a reference to a parameter the parties fill in. Rendered texts contain no numbers other than the parties’ own values. Every Term page ends “No parameter has a default value (A6)”, as on UP:Admin-Fee. In the same way, rules name roles, never particular organizations (Axiom A7), and Term texts read the same whichever party reads them (Overview §7.4).

On the second question, the language is built so that parties need not reveal prices to verify anything. Prices, rates, quantities and free text can each be replaced by a Seal, a salted hash commitment that proves a value exists and has not changed without revealing it. The salt stops anyone guessing a value by trying candidates. The value is disclosed separately, only to the parties who need it, and an evaluator without it reaches the same verdict on validity as one with it (Domain §3, Axiom A10). One exception: a value an agent sets within a numeric Mandate limit must be left unsealed, so it is visible to whoever holds that agreement’s record (Domain §10, rule 6).

What A6 does not do matters as much:

  • It does not make any conduct lawful. Parties can write any price they agree, and what they agree, and with whom, is assessed under the ordinary rules. GPO joint purchasing is generally analyzed under the rule of reason, and since the 2023 withdrawals described above there is no agency safety zone.
  • It binds the language, not the tools around it. The optional Fingerprint Coordination convention, which sits outside the language, pools parameter statistics across agreements and can suggest moving an outlying value toward a cohort median. It never counts sealed values, suppresses statistics drawn from fewer than a minimum number of agreements, and never suggests changes to price or tier Terms. Pooling terms across competitors is still exactly the kind of activity antitrust counsel should review before anyone does it.

Records as evidence

  • Tamper-evident by construction. Every agreement, event and state is identified by the hash of its canonical form. Each event commits to the hash of the state before it, so the record is a single chain, and no event can alter a past state: a correction is a new event (Axioms A3, A5).
  • Authentication. In federal court, data copied from an electronic file can be self-authenticating if a qualified person certifies that it was identified by a process of digital identification, such as matching hash values (Fed. R. Evid. 902(14), with the certification procedure of Rule 902(11)). A matching hash goes to authenticity only. It says nothing about the truth of the contents or about other objections.
  • Facts are claims. Facts about the world, such as a purchase or a member’s eligibility, enter only as signed attestations. The language records that a party claimed something and, where a Term requires it, that another party acknowledged it. It never treats a claim as true (Axiom A11).
  • Time is asserted. Each event’s time is its author’s claim. The chain requires times never to go backward, and counterparties’ acknowledgments limit backdating in practice, but there is no trusted clock (Design notes §4).

Limits counsel should know

  • The specification is a working draft (UP/0.1-draft). Until version 1.0, a change can alter what is valid. An agreement pins one version and the exact hash of every Term it uses, so a later change to the library never changes the meaning of an existing agreement (Axiom A1, Overview §9).
  • No Term has yet been reviewed by counsel from any segment. Each Term page records its review status under “Provenance”.
  • A party is its key, and this draft has no key rotation: a lost key means amending every agreement that party is in (Design notes §4).
  • The language does not decide disputes or replace courts. It gives the parties shared, verifiable evidence of what was agreed and what state it is in (Overview §7.2).
  • Every example party and value on this site is hypothetical.
